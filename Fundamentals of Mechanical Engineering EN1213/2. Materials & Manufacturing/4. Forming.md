Forming involves plastic flow in the solid state
Advantage:
- Fast production
- Economic material usage
- Produces high strength components due to
	- Work hardening
	- Grain regining
	- Defect closure
Disadvantage:
- High for normally required
- Dies are expensive
- Better surface finish and tolerance than casting, but worse then machining
Friction
- Consumes up to 50% of the energy put into the process
- Causes die wear
Lubrication therefore required
- Hot working : Solid lubrication
- Cold working : Solid lubrication

Advantages of cold working
- Good surface finish
- Better dimensional control
- Higher strength
Disadvantages of cold working
- High forces required
- Residual stress
- Limited deformation ratio achievable

Challenges
- Yield point is crucial
- Spring back can be a problem 
Anisotropy should be considered:
- Most materials are not isotropic, often as a result of processing
- r = εw / εt
- Isotropic: r0 = r45 = r90

Hot forging
- Shape created by hammering
- Performed at elevated temperature
- Slight excess of metal used
- Machining allowance needed

Hot rolling
- Used for producing long length of uniform cross section
- Friction drives the material through rollers
- 4-high setup typically used to maintain uniform strip thickness

Cold rolling
- Usually follows a hot rolling operation
- Ground rolls give
	- Good surface finish
	- Good thickness control
- Pickling used to remove oxide scale
- Work-hardening: often desirable

Extrusion
- Material pushed through shaped die
- Can be used to produce complex parts with constant cross section
- Can be carried out hot or cold
- Normally used with non-ferrous alloys

Rod, Tube & Wire Drawing
- Material is pulled through shaped die
- Good surface finish and accuracy
- Work hardening often desirable
- Drawing limit: Yield strength of the drawn material

Sheet metal working
Plain Bending
- No surface area increase
- Bending about linear axis
Roll bending
- Can be used for thin or thick plate and profiles
- Cold or hot roll forming possible
Straightening
- Deform slightly beyond elastic limit to straighten
- Roll straightening
Shearing
- Same mechanism as scissors
Square shearing
- Whole surface cut at once
- Conventional linear cutter
- Slitting: Continuous square cutting
Blanking
- Uses shaped punch and die
- Blanking removed part kept
- Piercing: Removed part discarded
Spinning
- Flat disc to conical shape of uniform thickness
Flow turning
- Like spinning, but variation in thickness
Deep or shallow draing
- For deep drawing Length > Diameter
- Hot drawing: plate thinning
- Cold drawing: less thinning
- Very cheap and fast for high volume
Hydroforming
- Die is replaced by rubber diaphragm backed by hydraulic pressure
- As punch moves upwards hydrostatic pressure forces blank around punch
Superplastic forming
- Superplastic material:
	- Special fine-grained structure
	- Al and Zn best results to date
	- Vaccum or pressure used
