Surface Tension (σ [N/m])
- The property of liquid which enables insects to walk on the surface of water, σ is defined as the force per unit length, so unites are N/m.

Fluid Properties
	Surface tension
- Its effect is to reduce the surface to a minimum
- Thus, drops of liquid tend to be spherical, causing for small droplets an increase of internal pressure.
- The forces caused by internal pressure are F = p x πr^2
- The forces due to surface tension around the perimeter is F = 2πr x σ
- Thus, p = 2σ/r
- If r is very small the value of pressure becomes very large. For small bubbles in a liquid, if this pressure is greater than the pressure of vapour, the bubble will collapse.
- The incorrect calculation of surface tension can cause serious errors in hydraulic scale models
- Surface tension can be reduced with detergents

Capillarity
- If a fine tube, open at both ends, is lowered vertically into a liquid which wets the tube, the level of the liquid will rise in the tube.
- If the liquid does not wet the tube, the level of liquid in the tube will be depressed below the level of the free surface.
- The atmospheric pressure is the same inside and outside the tube. Thus, the only force opposing to the upward pull is the weight of the liquid column. Being at rest, there will not by any shear stresses.
