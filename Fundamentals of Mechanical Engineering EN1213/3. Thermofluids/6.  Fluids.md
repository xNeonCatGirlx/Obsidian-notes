Quantities used in heat transfer:
- Force [N] (Newton)  
- Mass [kg] (kilogram mass)  
- Time [s] (second)  
- Length [m] (meter)  
- Temperature [K] or [°C] (Kelvin or Centigrades)  
- Energy [J] (joule)  
- Power [W] (watt)  
- Thermal Conductivity [W/m°C]or [W/mK]  
- Heat Transfer Coefficient [W/m2°C] or [W/m2K]  
- Specific Heat [J/kg°C] or [J/kgK]  
- Heat Flux [W/m2]

Solid vs Fluids Categorisation
- **Solids** are usually the most dense, and have the most determinate structure. Application of forces will not readily change volume or shape.
- **Solids** tend to deform or bend their shapes when we interact with them
- Shear forces acting on solid product an elastic change of shape which does not continue to increase with time, and which only remains so long as the force is applied.
- The main characteristic of fluids, is their tendency to flow when we interact with them
- Liquids generally have slightly lower density than solids, and looser structure
- Liquid volume cannot be easily changed, but the shape will change, and continue to change under small forces so long as the force is applied.

Fluids Categorisation
- Gases have a much lower density, and no coherent structure
- It is easy to change both volume and shape for gases
- Both liquids and gases are termed "Fluids" the main differences being increased density and viscosity in the case of liquids, and the high compressibility of gases
- The so-called IDEAL FLUID is defined as one which takes the observed properties of fluids to their extremes: It is assumed to be incompressible and inviscid. In most cases we'll use this definition
- Some materials show characteristics of both solids and liquids - visco-elastic materials. These are rather difficult to predict
