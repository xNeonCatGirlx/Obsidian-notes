Turning operations carried out on a “lathe”
Can be used for:  
- Turning  
- Facing  
- Parting off  
- Thread cutting  
- Boring  
- Knurling

![[Centre Lathe.png]]

- Lathe Bed: Base for the machine, holds the various components in precise alignment.  
- Headstock: Holds the bearings which support the spindle. Driven end of the lathe that gives the revolving motion to the workpiece. May also contain the gearbox used in thread cutting.  
- Tail stock: Moving holder for various tools including drills and centres, precisely aligned with headstock. Non-driven but can be manually positioned along the lathe bed.  
- Saddle: Holds cross slide, moves along the lathe bed. Saddle movement gives axial movement to the cutting tool. Moved manually or can be driven using power from the lathe.  
- Cross Slide: Moves transversely across the bed, holds the cutting tool and gives transverse tool movement. Again, can be driven manually or powered.  
- Compound Slide: Additional slide sometimes provided for further cutting control. Can be rotated to drive the tool at an angle to the workpiece.  
- Apron: Holds the controls for moving slides and controlling feeds.
- Lead Screw: Precision screw that drives saddle along bed at specific speeds. Only used when precision movement is required for thread cutting.  
- Feed Shaft: Used to drive saddle when not thread cutting. Prevents wear to the lead screw when such precise speeds are not required.

Turning:  
- Process of removing material from outer diameter of workpiece  
- Allows specified diameter to be created  
Facing:
- Process of removing metal from end of workpiece  
- Creates flat end surface
Parting off:
- Cuts workpiece to specified length  
- Parting tool driven transversely into workpiece  
Thread cutting:
- Creates threads by cutting a helical ridge on workpiece  
- Cutter driven at specific speed by lead screw (or controlled by motor in CNC lathes)  
- Gear ratio between the spindle gear and the lead screw changes the relative speed, and therefore the pitch of the thread.
Boring:
- Boring on a lathe to remove internal material from a workpiece  
- Straight and tapered holes can be created  
- Tool fixed, workpiece revolves (unlike in a boring machine)  
Knurling: 
- Creates a textured surface on workpiece by pressure or by cutting away material  
- Specialist tool imparts pattern
3 jaw “self-centring” chuck: 
- All jaws move at same time  
- Component automatically gripped in centre  
- Low accuracy (±0.25mm)
4 jaw “independent” chuck: 
- All jaws move independently  
- Allows workpiece to be manually aligned accurately  
- Can accommodate more complex shapes  
Collet chuck  
- Precision made, high accuracy (±0.025 mm)  
- Only accommodates specified size workpiece  
Turning between centres:  
- Mounts component centrally on its axis  
- Allows accurate transfer between processes  
- Drive dog used to impart motion, but not to locate the part

Casting
- A process in which a material such as metal or plastic in liquid form is poured into a mould and allowed to become hard, in order to make parts or products
- The component produced is also known as "a casting"
- Must allow for shrinkage, machining and distortion
- Numerous casting methods available
- Varying costs, accuracy, surface finish dependent on material and process
- Process can use 'expendable' or 'reusable' moulds
- Most widely used casting process
- Nearly all casting alloys can be send cast
- VersatileL parts range from small to very large
Advantages
- Production of final shape in a single step
- Complex geometries possible
- Low cost per component
Disadvantages
- Rough surface finish
- Poor dimensional accuracy
- Low strength
- High setup and energy costs

Sand casting requires a pattern: 
- Duplicate shape of part
- Can be made from: wood, plastic, metal
- Must account for shrinkage once cooled
- Sand mould is created from the pattern

Components of a casting mould:
- Cope & Drag
- Pouring cup
- Runner
- Riser 
- Gate
- Cores

• Riser: Liquid metal reservoir  
• Core: For creating hollow parts  
• Core print: Supports core  
• Draft required on pattern to allow pattern removal

Required properties of sand
- Refractoriness - withstand temperature
- Cohesiveness - allows mould to breathe
- Collapsibility - breaks up easily after cooling
- Availability - costs

Expendable moulds
- Mould created from a pattern
- Broken down to remove solidified cast component
- Mould material sometimes reusable
Reusable moulds
- Moulds usually machined directly from metal
- Hist cost, but can be reused after removing cast componenet

Simplifying part design improves "castability"
- Simplifies mould making
- Reduce the need for ores
- Improves casting stregth

Machining sllowances
- Casting tolerances are often insufficient for final product requirements
- Areas of the casting therefore need machining to the required dimensions
- Additional material is left on the casting where machining is nessary
