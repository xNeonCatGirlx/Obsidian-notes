Cutting methods:
Up-cutting
- Tool sharpness important due to force involved
Down-cutting
- Good surface finish achieved
- Less power consumed
- Backlash a major problem
- High rigidity required
Backlash
- Mechanical systems require clearances for movement
- Unwanted/uncontrolled movement in system
- Wear increases backlash

Straddle milling
- Multiple cutters spaced to cut both sides of workpiece at once
- Precise setup required
- Imporved processing speed
![[Pasted image 20261020221335.png]]

Gang milling
- Multiple cutters grouped together to form surface
- Long and expensive setup and maintenance costs
- Improved processing times and alignment
- Setup can be maintained for batches
![[Pasted image 20261020221635.png]]

Copy/Profile milling
- Machine follows original part to generate copied profile
- Bullnose or 'copy cutter' used
![[Pasted image 20261020221835.png]]

Duplex milling
- Allows simultaneous working of both sides of workpiece
- Motion replicated on both sides
![[Pasted image 20261020222352.png]]

Plano Mill
- Allows multiple indenpendent actions to be carried out at once
- Can by automated using CNC control
![[Pasted image 20261020223052.png]]

Drilling
- Two cutting edges
	- Cutting forces balanced
	- Torque + axial force applied
- Two flute
	- Material removal
- Chisel Tip
- Low Cost
- Efficient
- Poor heat removal
- Poor accuracy/finish
- Swarf removal difficult on deep holes
	- Depth > 5x diameter
- Process usually requires
	- Centre dirll
	- Pilot drill
	- Finish to required size, or undersize and ream

Broaching
- Used to produce special shaped holes, slots, and external surfaces
- Fast, low cost operation
- Operates like a saw balde - successive teeth remove material
- Tool very high cost
- Excellent repeatability
- Machining completed in one pass
