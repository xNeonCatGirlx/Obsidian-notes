Mechanics is the physical science that deals with the behaviour of bodies under the influence of forces. 
The course assumes its all rigid bodies

![[Statics in a nutshell.png]]

**A rigid body is a body which does not deform under the influence of forces.**

- Position - Defined relative to "fixed" reference
- Time - Position may vary with time in dynamic situations
- Mass - Quantity of matter making up a body
- Force - Action of one body on another means of forces

1. Forces can be added according to the Parallelogram Law
2. The law of Transmissibility: Point of application of a force can be moved along its line of action
3. Newton’s 1st Law: A force acting on a body changes its state of rest or of uniform motion in a straight line
4. Newton’s 2nd Law: The rate of change of momentum of a body is proportional to the force acting on it
5. Newton's 3rd Law: Forces of interaction between bodies are equal in magnitude, share the same line of action, and have opposite senses.
6. Newton's law of Gravitation: Every particle of matter in the universe attracts every other particle with a force that is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their center

- Force
A force represents the action of one body on another and may be transmitted by actual **Physical contact**, or by gravity and electromagnetic forces **without contact**.
Defined in terms of:
1. Position
2. Magnitude
3. Direction

- External force
Represent the action of one body on another. These are the forces that determine the external behaviour of the body
- Internal force
If a rigid body consists of several parts the forces holding these parts together are also called internal forces. Equivalent sets of external forces cause the same behaviour in the rigid body but may result in the internal forces
- Moment
Moment of a force about a point provides a measure of the turning effect on the body from an applied force.
- Varignon's theorem
The moment of a force about any axis is equal to the sum of the moments of its components about that axis.
